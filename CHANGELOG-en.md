- **1.19.0** Model roundup: GPT-6 at OpenAI, Gemini 4 Hydrogen at Google, Grok 4.7 at xAI; `--use_antigravity --eco` translates again; reasoning effort is configured for GPT-6 and Grok models (2026-11-15):

  - **`--use_antigravity --eco` translates again.** Antigravity removed Gemini 3.7 Flash from its catalog: `gemini-3.7-flash-low`, the model for `--eco`, is rejected there, and every translation was failing before the first segment. `--eco` switches to `gemini-3.8-flash-low`, the only tier below the default. Measured on November 15 on this README: identical structure in Japanese, Hindi, and English, for 0.25 points of the 5-hour window compared to 0.32 at medium effort. The default does not change. Antigravity's catalog now includes Gemini 4 Hydrogen and Gemini 4 Argon, supported by `--model`: Hydrogen uses more quota than 3.8 Flash at equal effort, 0.95 points at medium effort, and Argon draws from its own weekly quota, of which this README takes 1.35%, which aipmt warns about. A name without effort, such as `gemini-4-hydrogen`, is rejected before the call, alongside existing suffixes. Revalidated on agy 1.5.9, which now launches its headless sessions in Auto mode: the translation agent remains tool-free.

  - **`--use_gemini` switches to Gemini 4 Hydrogen.** Released on November 12, it is the only Gemini 4 in the free tier, and Google provides a migration guide from Gemini 3.8 Flash to it. Measured on November 15 on this README in Japanese, at the same time: identical structure for both, in 72 seconds versus 108. It costs $1.50 and $7.50 per million input and output tokens: this is the price that Gemini 3.8 Flash will adopt on January 1, 2027, at the end of its introductory pricing of $0.75 and $3.75. To keep that price until then, `--model gemini-3.8-flash` remains supported. Gemini 4 Argon and Carbon, the premium models, run via `--model`; they cost $2 and $10, then $4 and $20 starting December 1. All three Gemini 4 models reject reasoning `minimal`, like 3.8 Flash, and aipmt automatically steps up to the next level. `--eco` keeps Gemini 3.1 Flash Lite.

  - **OpenAI switches to GPT-6.1 Terra, and `--eco` to GPT-6 Luna.** Both cost half as much as their predecessors: $1 and $5 per million tokens compared to $2 and $12 for Terra, $0.10 and $0.50 compared to $0.20 and $1.20 for Luna. Measured on November 15 on this README in Japanese: four out of four translations with zero discrepancies for each, whereas GPT-5.6 Luna, the previous budget option, lost bold formatting once out of six times. GPT-6.1 Terra is slower than GPT-5.6 Terra: 190 to 245 seconds, compared to 116. OpenAI has announced no deprecation for GPT-5.6 models, which remain supported via `--model`. `--use_codex --eco` also switches to GPT-6 Luna, measured once on subscription: this README in Japanese, zero discrepancies, in 301 seconds.

  - **Reasoning effort is sent to GPT-6 and Grok models.** aipmt previously only configured reasoning for GPT-5 models: a GPT-6 or Grok model via API key reasoned at its default level on every segment, including `--eco`. Measured on a twenty-line guide: Grok 4.7 consumed 695 reasoning tokens for 113 translation tokens, and Grok 4.3, the model for `--eco`, used 373 for 103. GPT-6 Luna, without configuration, lost an inline code snippet or bold formatting three out of seven times; with reasoning turned off, it delivered four out of four READMEs without discrepancy. aipmt now sends reasoning disabled in `--eco`, medium effort to GPTs, and low effort to Groks: on this README, Grok 4.7 produces the same structure at low effort, in 190 seconds, as at medium effort, in 711. A model that rejects disabled reasoning, like Grok 4.7 or GPT-6.1 Sol, receives low effort instead of reverting to its default, most expensive setting. The xAI rejection, whose message is simply "Error code: 400", is now detected in the error body. `--reasoning_effort` still forces a specific value.

  - **`--use_grok` switches to Grok 4.7.** It costs the same as Grok 4.6, $2 and $6 per million tokens. Measured on this README in Japanese: identical structure, in 190 seconds, whereas Grok 4.6 takes 372 at medium effort. `--eco` keeps Grok 4.3, the only Grok model supporting disabled reasoning: three out of three translations written in about 3 minutes, two of which had an extra inline code element; without this setting, 1.18.0 wrote one out of two, in 5 minutes, with the other rejected for a lost code snippet.

  - **The context window table follows provider catalogs.** Added Claude Fable 5.2, GPT-6.1 Terra, all three Gemini 4 models, Mistral Large 4—now referenced by the alias `mistral-large-latest` with one million tokens—and the Antigravity identifiers recorded on November 15. Removed those Antigravity no longer serves.

- **1.18.0** Gemini switches to 3.8 Flash, retries transient outages, and no longer waits indefinitely; Python 3.11 minimum and Python 3.15 tested; the new major Gemini SDK release is validated (2026-11-15):

  - **`--use_gemini` switches to Gemini 3.8 Flash.** Google deprecated Gemini 3.7 Flash on October 8: it disappeared from AI Studio, and its endpoint serves Gemini 3.8 Flash in its place. The aipmt default was therefore naming a model that was no longer the one doing the translation. On November 14, on the free API tier, about twenty calls sent to 3.7 Flash exhausted the daily quota of 3.8 Flash. Pricing and reasoning tiers remain unchanged, and `--model gemini-3.7-flash` remains supported. Measured on November 15 on the free tier, a short file translated twice with identical structure: 3.8 Flash rejects reasoning `minimal`, like 3.7, and accepts the next tier, but it took 4 and then 8 minutes, when Google reported it was in high demand; the previous evening, `--eco` responded in one second. The model for `--eco`, Gemini 3.1 Flash Lite, will be retired on May 7, 2027: its designated successor, Gemini 3.5 Flash Lite, will be evaluated before taking its place.

  - **A transient Gemini outage no longer costs the entire file.** Google's SDK does not retry anything by default: on the first "503 high demand", aipmt would abandon the file, whereas the same failure has been retried on Antigravity since 1.17.1. A 503, a 429, or a timeout now retries the call up to three times, after 10, 20, and then 40 seconds. Depleted prepaid credits or rejected requests still fail immediately.

  - **An unresponsive Gemini call no longer stalls aipmt.** The SDK used to wait indefinitely: on November 14, a translation hung for over five minutes on a silent server until it was aborted. Each attempt is now timed out after 5 minutes, then retried as a transient outage: a silent server causes the segment to fail in about twenty minutes at most. For this, aipmt requires a recent enough version of the Gemini SDK to retry timeouts, which pip updates as needed.

  - **Gemini retries are now visible.** The Google SDK logs its retries to an unmonitored logger: four minutes of waiting on a small file looked indistinguishable from a freeze. Every retry is now logged, showing the error and the delay: on November 15, a "504 DEADLINE_EXCEEDED" after 5 minutes was successfully retried 10 seconds later. Warnings previously displayed by the SDK continue to show.

  - **google-genai major version bump, validated with live API calls.** This SDK no longer bundles `httpx`, which is now optional, and moves to `httpx2`. aipmt imports neither, and its translation call remains unchanged. A Markdown guide translated in `--eco` using `gemini-3.1-flash-lite` with this version maintains structural parity with the source. A single test constructed an Anthropic error using `httpx`: under Python 3.11 and 3.12, nothing installed it anymore, and the suite failed on import. It now uses `httpx2`, matching Anthropic's and Mistral's SDKs.

  - **Python 3.11 becomes the minimum version.** Python 3.10 reached end-of-life on October 1, 2026, and the new major version of google-genai, released on November 6, requires at least Python 3.11; six other packages in the lockfile also require it. On Python 3.10, pip only installs up to 1.17.1, the last version supporting it.

  - **Python 3.15 is tested.** The test suite runs on every Python version from 3.11 to 3.15, with the package installed and dependencies resolved against their public boundaries. SonarCloud analysis, dependency checks, and PyPI publishing now run on Python 3.15.

  - **Dependencies.** In the lockfiles, `anthropic`, `openai`, and `mistralai` received minor updates submitted by Dependabot and merged automatically. `pyasn1`, pulled in by Google authentication, received fixes for four high-severity security vulnerabilities involving quadratic-time decodings that crafted payloads could exploit to cause long stalls. PyPI installations already fetched the patched version. `httpx` and `httpcore` were removed from the lockfiles as no packages require them anymore. The GitHub actions for Python setup, PyPI publishing, and SonarCloud analysis were updated manually.

- **1.17.1** Grok CLI switches to Grok 4.7, the only model it still accepts; transient Antigravity outages and Grok token refreshes no longer cause files to fail (2026-10-21):

  - **`--use_grok_cli` translates again, using Grok 4.7.** Starting with version 1.0.52, the Grok CLI rejects Grok 4.6 and Grok 4.5, aipmt's quality and `--eco` models ("unknown model id"): every translation failed on the very first segment. On October 19, Grok 4.6 was still translating. Grok 4.7 becomes the default and, with no second model available under the subscription, `--eco` has no effect on this backend. The xAI API still serves Grok 4.6 and Grok 4.3: `--use_grok` remains unchanged. Re-tested on the 1.17.0 test suite, a guide and a short `--news` post containing code inside an English quote: three out of three translations succeeded, matching the results of other providers.

  - **Antigravity retries transient service outages.** On October 20, agy returned "API error (attempt 2): UNAVAILABLE (code 503)" on the fourth segment of a CHANGELOG after retrying once internally. Nothing indicated the failure was retryable: aipmt aborted the file after consuming the quota of the first three segments, even though a manual retry succeeded. Codes 502, 503, and 504, which agy already retries internally, now trigger segment retries after 30 and 60 seconds. An exhausted quota window remains an immediate failure, and when agy itself reports whether the failure is retryable, its determination takes precedence.

  - **Grok CLI preflight checks no longer mistake token refreshes for disconnections.** Before translating, aipmt checks the session using `grok models`, which incurs no usage cost. However, the call refreshing an expired token itself responds with "You are not authenticated.", and the subsequent call succeeds: aipmt used to recommend re-authenticating with `grok login` when simply retrying was enough. It now queries again five seconds later before concluding. Observed on October 19, reproduced on October 21.

  - **Retry notifications display the reason.** Codex, Grok CLI, Antigravity, OpenCode, and Claude Code used to report every retry as a "rate limit", including 503s—leading users to suspect quota limits. The notification now includes the actual error returned by the CLI.

  - **Dependencies.** In the lockfiles, `openai` received its minor update, and `certifi` received a fix for a critical security vulnerability removing emSign roots whose intermediate keys were compromised. PyPI installations already fetched the patched version.

- **1.17.0** Claude 5.5 via the API, Codex on GPT-6.1-Sol: Sonnet 5.5, which rejected disabled thinking, is now usable for translation and becomes the default model; Haiku 5.5 takes `--eco` mode; Codex switches to GPT-6.1-Sol and no longer drops segments that complete after reconnecting; Dependabot runs over weekends too (2026-10-20):

  - **`--use_claude` translates using Claude Sonnet 5.5, Opus 5.5, and Fable 5.1.** aipmt previously disabled Claude thinking using `thinking: {"type": "disabled"}`, which these three models reject: HTTP 400 on the first segment, as observed in live calls. aipmt now sends the lowest setting accepted by each model, based on capabilities published by the Anthropic API: `between_tools` for Sonnet 5.5, which disables its initial thinking phase, and no setting for Opus 5.5 and Fable 5.x, allowing them to reason as needed. Any new model will, on the first rejection, try settings from most conservative to most permissive, then retain the working configuration for subsequent segments—the same approach already used for Gemini. Any other error bubbles up directly. All four 5.x models are added to the context window table at one million tokens.

  - **Claude Sonnet 5.5 becomes the default model for `--use_claude`.** Measured by aipmt itself across both benchmark documents from the README—the dense tech watch article in `--news` and this README at revision 1.14.0—across fourteen languages each: 28 out of 28 translations written with zero structural discrepancies, no segments re-run, internal links preserved, English blockquotes retained verbatim, and, in English, French translation rows omitted by the model itself. Claude Sonnet 5 achieved 11 and 12 out of 14 via the API on a longer revision of this README, and 13 and 13 via Claude Code. Pricing remains unchanged: $2 and $10 per million input and output tokens, totaling $9.21 for all 28 translations.

  - **`--eco` switches to Claude Haiku 5.5, thinking included.** Measured on the same documents: with thinking disabled, Haiku 5.5 leaves whole paragraphs in French, which the untranslated content check rejects—only 9 out of 28 translations completed. With adaptive thinking enabled, it completed 26, 23 of which had zero discrepancies; two README files were rejected (in Arabic and Hindi) due to missing inline code blocks on return. Claude Haiku 4.5, the previous default, benchmarked for the first time via the API, completed all 28 but only 21 without discrepancies: on the article, it elevated a header to level 1 across seven languages and left it in French in Japanese, bypassing all checks. Haiku 5.5 costs $0.57 for all 28 translations, down from $3.67, and runs twice as fast; aipmt therefore lets it reason.

  - **Codex no longer drops segments completed after reconnecting.** When the server responds with a 429, `codex exec` reconnects automatically and logs each retry in its JSON stream via a `error` event ("Reconnecting... 2/5") before resuming the turn, which can still succeed. aipmt previously treated this initial `error` as a failure and aborted the file: three of the fourteen failures during a regeneration of this repository on October 19 carried this message. Only the turn completion now determines status, `turn.completed` or `turn.failed`, as specified in the Codex 0.162.1 source code. A 429 that exhausts Codex retries—which only surfaces as text—is also detected: aipmt waits and retries the segment instead of abandoning the file. An exhausted subscription quota remains an immediate failure.

  - **`--use_codex` switches to GPT-6.1-Sol.** It is the first model in the catalog that Codex serves to a ChatGPT account, and aipmt still preferred GPT-5.6-Sol. Measured on October 19 on this CHANGELOG in German, one translation per model, launched at the same time: GPT-6.1-Sol in 527 seconds, segments from 46 to 70 seconds, identical source structure; GPT-5.6-Sol in 906 seconds, served that evening in fits and starts, up to 211 seconds for a segment, and 429s as soon as four translations ran in parallel. The GPT-6 family no longer triggers the "unusual model" warning. GPT-5.6-Sol remains accessible via `--model gpt-5.6-sol`, and `--eco` keeps GPT-5.6-Luna.

  - **Dependabot runs every day, including weekends.** Set to `daily`, it only ran Monday through Friday: a version that exceeded its three-day window on a Friday, after the morning run, waited until Monday, and the daily freshness check, calibrated to one run per day, would have flagged it as overdue on Sunday. On October 18, google-genai 2.30.1 escaped this by 1 hour and 35 minutes. Dependabot now follows a daily cron schedule, and the test aligning the check's delay with its schedule counts three days for `daily`: returning to it without extending the delay causes it to fail.

  - **The split purity checker is removed**, as planned since 1.13.0. It compared the package to the snapshot taken during splitting, and each code modification had to declare its discrepancies there. Its most useful check remains: `check-release-ready.sh` rejects an untracked Python module in git, invisible to both hooks and CI.

  - **Two defects flagged by SonarCloud are fixed**: the function that sorts code tokens from a quote `--news`, which was too complex, is split into two, and an exception test now calls only one function within its block.

  - **Dependencies up to date since 1.16.3.** In the pinned versions, `anthropic`, `openai`, `google-genai`, `mistralai`, and `python-dotenv` received their minor and patch updates, proposed by Dependabot and merged automatically; the publishing and SonarCloud analysis actions were bumped manually. The two questions left open by automatic merging are settled: it works without additional permissions, and it does not trigger any push workflow — the daily check of `main` serves as a replacement.

- **1.16.3** End of file: an API translation preserves the final newline of its source, and Mistral SDK 3.0 is validated (2026-10-04):

  - **An API translation preserves the final newline of its source.** API providers — OpenAI, Anthropic, Gemini, Mistral — strip surrounding whitespace from the model's response. On the last segment, this cleanup stripped the source's final newline: a translated file without a translator note ended without a newline, which git flags and formatters fix on every run. Seen while validating Mistral SDK 3.0, on a guide ending with a newline whose translation no longer had one. The translator note added one back, and CLI providers — Codex, Grok CLI, Antigravity, Claude Code, OpenCode — copy the end of the segment: only an API translation without a note was affected. Translation now returns the source's final newline, regardless of the provider, and adds none to a source that lacks one.

  - **Mistral: SDK 3.0 validated by a live call.** `mistralai` 3.0.0 replaces the HTTP library `httpx` with `httpx2`; imports and the translation call remain unchanged. A fresh installation of aipmt was already receiving it, since dependencies have no upper version cap, but no live call had tested it yet: a Markdown guide translated via the Mistral API using SDK 3.0 retains a structure identical to the source. The pinned versions for development switch to this SDK, and retry tests supply it with a `httpx2` client, the one it uses in production.

- **1.16.2** Code protection: code quoted inside a blockquote `--news` and an indented block in a list no longer cause translation to be rejected across all languages (2026-10-03):

  - **Code quoted inside a blockquote no longer blocks translation.** In `--news` mode, aipmt protects inline code before setting aside English blockquotes, which are restored as-is. Code placed inside a quote was therefore set aside with it: the model never saw its token, and validation flagged it as lost across all languages. The author's daily news watch came to a halt there on October 2: a command `/plugin enable …` quoted in a tweet caused the probe language to be rejected twice, leaving the other thirteen never launched. Tokens that a quote took along are now resolved within the set-aside text. For an English target, the French translation line of a blockquote is no longer sent to the model: the prompt instructed it to delete it, and the code it contained was then counted as lost even though the model had complied. The published output does not change. Finally, restoring a blockquote no longer reinterprets its backslashes: a quoted `\n` remains `\n`. Verified end-to-end on the article that had failed: English and German translated on the first try by `gpt-6.1-sol`, structure identical to published versions.

  - **An indented code block in a list is protected.** CommonMark allows an indented block fence, which is typical for a block documenting a numbered list step: it belongs to the list item, so it is indented. Anchored at column 0, the protection pattern missed them all: measured on an actual README, 8 blocks seen out of 14. The other six went to the model as prose and came back identical, since they are commands, and the guard rejecting a verbatim-copied translation rejected the file for all languages, with a message naming the symptom and never the cause. The pattern now accepts an indented fence, and the round-trip between protection and restoration returns the document identically, indentation included.

- **1.16.1** Internal links: the anchor token leaves parentheses to the link, and a translated link is no longer silently duplicated (2026-09-27):

  - **An internal link no longer breaks without anything noticing.** To protect a section link, `[texte](#ancre)`, aipmt replaced its parenthesized portion, parentheses included, with a token: the model received brackets followed by a bare token, an apparently broken link, and it would sometimes "fix" it. Restoration then yielded `[texte]((#ancre))`, a link leading nowhere, without any guard flagging it. The defect recurred with every regeneration of this repository's translations — in Spanish, Korean, then Italian for 1.15.0, in German for 1.16.0 — with every Gemini Flash tested, and even with Claude Sonnet and Haiku. The token of a Markdown link now keeps its parentheses: the model receives a valid link, which it no longer has any reason to fix, and restoration also accepts a token from which it might have removed parentheses. HTML anchors retain a bare token. Verified end-to-end with the most fragile model, `gemini-3.8-flash-low`, on a README containing a parenthesized link: without the fix, README rejected, three duplicated links, and one segment rerun; with it, three intact links out of three, no reruns. Then on this README, in German, Japanese, and Hindi via `gemini-3.8-flash-medium`, and in German via Claude Code with `sonnet`: structure identical to the source, the three internal links intact everywhere.

  - **Tests can no longer modify the repository running them.** When pushed from a linked worktree, git passes the variable pointing to the repository to its hooks, and the pre-push hook runs the tests: a test's `git init`, intended for a temporary directory, reset the actual repository, which was suddenly declared without a working tree. Tests running git now strip any `GIT_*` variable from their environment, and a regression test pointing this variable to a decoy repository verifies this.

- **1.16.0** Provider `--use_claude_code`: translate using the quota of a Claude Pro or Max subscription, via the official CLI `claude -p` — no pay-as-you-go route, and every call proves it (2026-09-26):

  - **Eleventh path: Claude subscription, without pay-as-you-go billing.** `--use_claude_code` drives the official Claude Code binary in `-p` mode, on the same foundation as Codex, Grok CLI, and Antigravity — dedicated process group, safe shutdown on timeout, Ctrl-C, or terminal closure. Not to be confused with `--use_claude`, Anthropic's pay-as-you-go API. aipmt never reads the token.

  - **No pay-as-you-go route, verified at three levels.** The subprocess environment is an allowlist: no API key, no token, no cloud provider, and no markers from the Claude Code session from which aipmt might be launched — the one used to write this provider exported fifteen. The preflight check, without a model turn, requires `claude auth status` to show the subscription login without a Console key, and `/usage` to show the phrase confirming the subscription. Each call must then confirm it in its initialization event — `apiKeySource: none`, forced environment properly received, model from the requested family, no fast mode — and in its quota report: paid "extra usage" halts the translation immediately.

  - **Quota is shared with Claude Code sessions, and the provider conserves it.** Each call reports the utilization of the 5-hour and weekly windows; beyond 80% (`AIPMT_CLAUDE_MAX_UTILIZATION`), no further segments are started. On the day of writing, the author's account week was already at 98%.

  - **Nothing from user configuration enters, nothing from the session remains.** Tool-free, personalization-free mode, user settings ignored, MCP servers and hooks disabled, private and disposable working directory, no transcripts saved. Measured with canaries: without `CLAUDE_CODE_DISABLE_ATTACHMENTS`, a `@chemin` in the document became an attachment, and the model returned a read call instead of the translation; with it, nothing leaks through. The `AGENTS.md`, `CLAUDE.md`, and hook canaries loaded and executed nothing.

  - **A segment starting with `/` did not reach the model.** Under `--disable-slash-commands`, Claude Code replies to it itself with "… isn't available in this environment.", with status `success`, without any turns. A newline before the segment sends it to the model; the output contract rejects in any case a response returned without a turn, in more than one turn, truncated, or by a model other than the one announced.

  - **Models: aliases, never a pinned identifier.** `sonnet` by default, at effort `low`, and in `--eco` as well — `--eco` therefore changes nothing on this path —, `opus` and `haiku` via `--model`; `fable` and `[1m]` variants are rejected because under `-p` they switch to paid credits without asking.

  - **Flaws are measured, not assumed.** Via `aipmt --use_claude_code` itself, four parallel translations on the two benchmark documents from the README — the dense news watch article, in `--news`, and this README at revision 1.14.0 —, fourteen languages each. `sonnet`, at effort `low`, writes all 28 translations, 26 without discrepancies: one extra bold word in Chinese on the article, and in Arabic on the README, a table row merged into the previous one, hidden by rendering. Internal links intact, English blockquotes copied as-is, and in English, French translation lines removed by the model without invented flags; 6 min 49 s median per language on the article, 2 min 20 s on the README. `opus` translates the README with zero discrepancies, 25% faster, but no articles: its guardrails halted the response in all fourteen languages on a biology news item. `haiku` writes everything — three section titles bumped to level 1 on the article, internal links in the English README duplicated —, but twice as slowly as `sonnet`: it reasons unavoidably, accounting for 61% of its output tokens, and its lower price yields only a 14% savings in API-equivalent cost. Hence `sonnet` in `--eco` as well. Effort is useless for translation: on a preliminary batch — this README and this CHANGELOG in English, Japanese, and Hindi —, `sonnet` produced zero reasoning tokens, `opus` at medium effort produced 1% of its output tokens, and medium effort gained nothing over low, neither in structure nor in duration. The `sonnet` run, 1.8 million source characters, consumed 9 percentage points of the 5-hour window and 2 of the weekly window on the author's Max subscription — an upper bound: the session running the benchmarks drew from the same quota.

  - **A rejection by Anthropic's guardrails is explicitly named.** Opus 5.5 has stricter guardrails than Sonnet, and Anthropic's error message warns that they sometimes flag work related to biological research. A news item covering 279 molecules generated for a binding site was enough. Claude Code then cuts the response, retries once, and the model returns "I can't continue this translation" with a success status, no error, over two turns: only the single-turn contract halted this response, with a message silent as to its cause. Sent alone, the news item is rejected by the API under the "bio" category. aipmt now recognizes these signs, names the guardrails and their category, does not retry — the same text would retrigger the same rejection — and recommends `--model sonnet`, which translated the item across all fourteen languages.

  - **Translations for this repository can use this path without exemptions.** `REGEN_PROVIDER=claude_code` is a subscription, not a billed API: it runs without `REGEN_ALLOW_PAID_API`, with four jobs — the test runs operated at four concurrent translations without a single rate-limit or connection-lock error —, after a single preflight check that consumes zero quota. The default remains Codex with `gpt-5.6-sol`.

  - **Terms of service stated plainly.** The Claude Code legal page does not prevent a user from connecting to the unmodified binary with their own subscription; it forbids third-party developers from routing requests through their users' Pro or Max credentials, and Anthropic prefers the API key for third-party tools, open-source projects included, reserving the right to bill their usage against paid credits. No text explicitly resolves the case of a distributed tool invoking the official binary: users commit their own accounts.

  - **Tests**: new file `tests/test_claude_code_provider.py` (58 tests), 100% statement and branch coverage of the module, without ever launching the real `claude` — stripped environment, non-existent binary, `PATH` without `claude`, test doubles that raise if invoked.

- **1.15.0** Provider `--use_antigravity`: translate using the quota of a Google AI Pro or Ultra subscription, via the official CLI `agy`; and, for all providers, Ctrl-C no longer leaves orphan agents running, an empty segment no longer fails the file, and a project's `.env` no longer determines the executed binary (2026-09-26):

- **Tenth provider path: Google subscription, no pay-per-use billing.** Anyone paying for Google AI Pro or Ultra can now have their translations counted against it instead of paying per token on the Gemini API. `agy`, the official Antigravity CLI, is the only path to this quota: Gemini CLI no longer serves Pro, Ultra, or free accounts since June 18, 2026, and the Antigravity SDK only authenticates via an API key or Google Cloud. `--use_antigravity` drives it in headless mode, like Codex and Grok CLI, on the same foundation — dedicated process group, `SIGTERM` then `SIGKILL` upon timeout, sanitized environment. Verified end-to-end on agy 1.2.11: a Markdown guide — front matter, bold, parenthesized link, inline code, indented code block in a list, table, blockquote — translated into English in 14 seconds, preflight included, identical structure to the source, translation note added; then, after review fixes, into German in 19 seconds, identical structure, nothing written to `~/.gemini`.

  - **No paid route remains open, and every launch checks this.** agy supports other routes besides the subscription — API key, Google Cloud project, gateway, paid AI credits — and several environment variables silently switch it over. Measured: `AGY_GATEWAY_URL` sends the document to a third-party gateway, `AGY_ADC_AUTH` switches to a billed Google Cloud project, `CLOUD_CODE_URL` redirects the authenticated endpoint to any address. The subprocess environment is therefore an allowlist, not a denylist: `PATH`, language and timezone, terminal, identity, machine proxies and certificates, and the session bus — what an `env -i` proved agy needs —, without any keys. A denylist based on prefix families (`AGY_`, `GOOGLE_`, `GEMINI_`, `CLOUD_CODE_`…), attempted first, missed some: a review found endpoint overrides in the binary's strings that no prefix covered, `AICODE_ENDPOINT_URL`, `UNLEASH_URL`, `GCE_METADATA_HOST`. Two settings escape the environment and can only be read via `agy -p /config`: paid AI credits that take over when quota is exhausted, and connecting via a Google Cloud project. A preflight that consumes zero quota — measured zero usage — refuses to translate if either is active or if an API key is configured, and a missing setting in `/config` counts as refusal for each of the three: an agy version that renames one must not pass a check that verified nothing. Finally, the log of each call must confirm subscription authentication (`authMethod=consumer`), and any other authentication method appearing there, under either spelling used by the binary, causes the response to be rejected: the call itself is what counts, not the configuration that was prepared.

  - **The translation agent has no tools, and its loading is proven on every call.** Each call writes, in a disposable directory, an agent formatted as an agy project agent: `tools: []`, `excludeDefaultComponents: true`, `inheritCustomizations: false`. About 800 input tokens instead of 12,400 with the default agent, and the third setting is not redundant: `excludeDefaultComponents` alone still let a workspace hook execute commands and inject text. The real trap lies elsewhere: a missing agent does not cause agy to fail. Return code 0, status `SUCCESS`, and it is the coding agent, with 57 active tools, that responds — to "The cat is sleeping.", it answered "Shh, let's not wake it up! 🐱💤 What can I help you with today?". Nothing on stdout or stderr: only the call log shows it. The response is therefore rejected if the log reports this fallback, and also if it lacks the positive marker confirming the agent was loaded — an evolution in the log format causes the translation to be rejected instead of silently dropping the guard. Each marker must occupy an entire log line, in the measured format — severity, date, time, thread, file, and line: the binary also contains a line that echoes the user's message into the log — never observed at the default log level across 78 real logs —, and a document quoting these messages must not be able to satisfy the guard in place of agy.

  - **The user's agy settings and history are untouched.** Each call runs with a private HOME, temporary directory, and working directory, deleted afterward. agy still authenticates via the system keychain, which aipmt never reads. Nothing is inherited: neither user permissions — which allowed the default agent to run `ls` and `git` without prompting —, nor global rules, plugins, MCP servers, hooks, or skills. And nothing is added to their history, where each call used to leave a conversation of about 200 KB, including translated text: the probe campaign had left about 95 conversations there and evicted 17 entries from a cache capped at 100. Verified: no files written to `~/.gemini/antigravity-cli`.

  - **Fail-closed refusal where isolation would not hold.** On Linux without a D-Bus session bus — SSH session, container, server —, agy stores its token in a file in `~/.gemini` instead of the keychain, as stated in its own changelog (1.1.3), and isolating each call hides this file. The refusal now occurs before any launch, with its actual cause, instead of waiting a minute for a login code followed by a reconnection suggestion that would solve nothing. On Windows, agy reads `USERPROFILE`, `APPDATA`, and `TEMP`, not `HOME` or `TMPDIR` — according to Go documentation and binary strings —: nothing there would isolate the call from user settings or history, and `--use_antigravity` is rejected there until measured. macOS passes, without having been measured; the billing guard, which reads the effective configuration, holds there.

  - **`exit 0` proves nothing, fourth CLI.** A response is only accepted with a zero return code, a JSON object with status `SUCCESS`, a compliant log, no rejected actions, no commands, no errors in the JSON, no truncation warnings on stderr, and non-empty text. `--print-timeout` is never used: upon expiration, agy returns partial output with `SUCCESS`, code 0; the timeout is external and kills the process group. A segment starting with `/commande`, `!commande`, or `@fichier` is translated, never executed or resolved — without `--disable-slash-commands`, "/help" was executed instead of translated. A blank segment no longer reaches agy, as the pipeline returns it as-is (see below), and remains rejected before the call as a last safety net: on empty input, agy opens its interactive interface with code 0. Retries follow the `retryable` field of the `AGY_ERROR` line that agy has written to stderr since 1.2.6: it already retries transient errors internally — 502, 503, 504, per-minute limit —, and it is the one that knows whether the remaining failure is still transient. In the absence of this field, no retry occurs when the message mentions exhaustion — at Google, `RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED` carries the same 429 code as a per-minute limit, but an exhausted 5-hour window does not recover in 90 seconds —, and otherwise retries only on rate limits. The response adopts the segment's line ending: agy always terminates its own with a newline, which, at boundaries, split a paragraph broken mid-sentence in two. A disconnected session yields a message advising to run `agy` to reconnect. Finally, `AGY_CLI_DISABLE_AUTO_UPDATE=true` prevents a fresh HOME from spawning an update checker on each call in its own session, beyond the reach of the timeout — the value `1` has no effect.

- **1.13.0** Provider `--use_openrouter`: a paid router to ~430 models, including open Chinese models (2026-09-05):

  - **Ninth provider path, shipped alongside the eighth.** 1.12.0 was not published on PyPI: both routers, OpenCode and OpenRouter, are released together. [OpenRouter](https://openrouter.ai) provides access with a single key to models that no other provider exposes here—Kimi, Qwen, DeepSeek, Z.ai—on a single pay-as-you-go credit balance. Since the endpoint is OpenAI-compatible, the client is the same as for xAI; **everything distinguishing this provider resides in a preflight**, and each of its rules stems from measurements on the API.

  - **A single model is served by dozens of hosts with different limits, and routing is blind to this.** Measured: 33 hosts for `z-ai/glm-5.2`, 23 for `z-ai/glm-5.3-flash`—including one capped at **2,048 output tokens**. One long translation out of 23 therefore ended up truncated, at random, without the slightest warning. The preflight inspects `/api/v1/models/{modèle}/endpoints`, discards hosts under 8,000 output tokens, those with degraded status, and those declaring no ceiling, then pins the remaining ones. `provider.only` **without** `allow_fallbacks: false` is merely a preference: the router falls back to a discarded host, rendering the pinning useless. If no host meets the limit, the command halts: translating anyway would mean accepting the silent truncation this preflight was built to prevent.

  - **Reasoning is billed at output rates and enabled by default on many models.** Same query on `z-ai/glm-5.2`, response "OK": **107 completion tokens at model default, 2 with reasoning disabled**. On a translation, where reasoning adds no value, this is an 18x multiplier on every segment of every file. It is therefore disabled by default. The **288 out of 431 models** that require it (`reasoning.mandatory`) return `400 « Reasoning is mandatory for this endpoint and cannot be disabled »`: for them, the preflight checks accepted effort levels and requests the lowest one (next bullet)—effort allocates a **percentage of `max_tokens`** that reasoning consumes first, meaning a randomly picked value shifts the blank-page risk instead of reducing it.

  - **A model that requires reasoning receives the lowest effort it accepts, and this was decided by measurement.** The initial choice was to send nothing, to avoid guessing for the model. Tested on `z-ai/glm-5.3-flash`, whose catalog default is `max`, this choice produced an output **truncated at 32,768 tokens** before the translation completed—two languages out of fourteen lost. Increasing the budget would not have helped: effort allocates a percentage of it, so reasoning grows alongside it. The provider therefore reads `supported_efforts` during preflight and requests the lowest one, falling back to "nothing" when the catalog reports no usable values. Counter-test on the failing language: previously failing on an exhausted budget, it now passes in 9 minutes with a structure identical to the source.

  - **An upstream host outage now bears its name.** The router normalizes this case into `finish_reason=error` with a null `native_finish_reason`—measured twice, across two languages, at exactly 750 seconds. The generic message led users to look for an error in the document or chunking; it now states that the failure is upstream and that retrying is often sufficient.

  - **`finish_reason=length` with empty output is not truncation.** It is the budget consumed by reasoning before the first usable character—measured at 15,850 reasoning tokens for 148 usable tokens. The two cases call for opposite actions: reducing segment size achieves nothing in the former. The message distinguishes them explicitly. Two other guards stem from measurements: the router returns **200 with a body containing only an error** when the upstream host fails (`choices[0]` raised an opaque `TypeError` that masked the message), and the context window is read from the catalog then written into `MODEL_TOKEN_LIMITS`—`DEFAULT_TOKEN_LIMIT` is false for 44 models in the catalog, including two capped at 4,095 tokens.

  - **`--model fournisseur/modèle` is required, and its format is validated before any network call.** OpenRouter is not a provider: the choice impacts price, licensing, and data handling, so it is not made on the user's behalf. The agentic CLI foundation falls back to `terminate` then `kill` where POSIX process groups do not exist, instead of letting an `AttributeError` bypass the timeout guard and prolonging the wait. The `429` marker in OpenCode is matched as a number rather than a substring, where an error identifier like `err_84290b` triggered 90 seconds of back-off before failing anyway. Finally, a non-canonical OpenRouter endpoint is displayed at preflight: a project `.env` is enough to set one up, and the real key would then be sent.
  - **Security: a project `.env` can no longer redirect API calls.** `find_dotenv(usecwd=True)` searches for the file starting from the current directory and its parents: an untrusted tree — a freshly cloned repository — could place `OPENROUTER_BASE_URL`, `XAI_BASE_URL`, or `OPENAI_BASE_URL` there (the latter read by the SDK itself) without knowing any key, and the real key, coming from the environment or user configuration, was then sent in the authorization header to a third-party server. The filter operates on a PATTERN rather than a list: identified across the installed SDKs, twelve routing variables are read, six of them by the Anthropic client alone — a hand-written list would have missed half of them. Therefore, any variable in `_BASE_URL`, `_API_BASE`, or `_ENDPOINT`, proxies, and certificate stores (pointing to a controlled authority makes an interceptor indistinguishable from a genuine server), as well as `XDG_CONFIG_HOME` and `APPDATA`, are rejected from the project layer: setting them amounted to deciding which file constitutes the user layer, thereby bypassing the filter indirectly. These variables are accepted only from the two layers controlled by the user, the exported environment and `~/.config/aipmt/.env`. The project layer is furthermore read WITHOUT interpolation: `load_dotenv` expands `${VAR}` by default, and an untrusted `.env` containing `NOM_ANODIN=${OPENAI_API_KEY}` copied the real key under a name that the subprocess pattern filtering does not recognize — it then entered the environment of `codex exec`, violating the stated invariant. Finally, the rejection only displays the variable name: a URL of the form `https://${CLE}@hôte/` leaked the interpolated key into the logs, even while rejecting it. Rejection is reported on stderr along with guidance — an enterprise relay is declared in the user configuration.
  - **Fix: the OpenRouter output envelope is computed for each call.** `context_length` covers input AND completion, and a fixed reserve calibrated on Latin text provides no boundary: measured with tokenizer `o200k_base`, 16,000 characters equal 3,200 tokens in French but 12,300 in Japanese and 17,500 in emoji. The budget is therefore derived from the text actually sent, upper-bounded by its UTF-8 byte count: across all byte-merging tokenizers — byte-level BPE, SentencePiece with byte fallback, the families used across the catalog — a token is worth at least one byte, and this is the only upper bound available here, where OpenRouter routes to dozens of unknown tokenizers. No average ratio worked — a supplementary plane ideograph drops to 1.33 bytes per token, a combining character to 1.00 —, and input plus output now fit within the window by design. A segment too dense for the selected model is rejected before the call rather than after billing.
