- **1.19.1** `--use_codex --eco` reverts to GPT-5.6 Luna, which consumes less quota; the path of a file located outside the source directory is displayed as provided (2026-11-15):

  - **`--use_codex --eco` reverts to GPT-5.6 Luna.** 1.19.0 had switched it to GPT-6 Luna based on a single measurement, as Codex's quota window ran out before the comparison. Re-run that same evening on this README in Japanese, reading the subscription quota before and after each translation: GPT-5.6 Luna yields two out of two translations with zero discrepancies, in 313 seconds, consuming 0 then 1 point of the 5-hour window; GPT-6 Luna yields three out of four without discrepancies, losing bold formatting once, just as fast, but for 3 points on each of the two measured translations. On the subscription, GPT-6 Luna therefore consumes at least three times as much quota without offering any benefit. It remains accepted via `--model gpt-6-luna`, and remains the model for `--eco` via the OpenAI API, where it costs half price.

  - **The path of a file located outside the source directory is displayed as provided.** With `--file`, `--source_dir` keeps its default value, and aipmt used to announce "Processing file: content/posts/../../README.md". It now displays the path as received; a file located under `--source_dir` keeps its relative form. Only the display changes.

  - **Gemini 3.5 Flash Lite does not yet replace Gemini's eco model.** Measured on this README in Japanese, three translations per setting: the model for `--eco`, Gemini 3.1 Flash Lite, yields two without discrepancies; Gemini 3.5 Flash Lite, its designated replacement, yields none at minimal reasoning and only one at low as well as medium levels, the others being rejected due to lost inline code or returned with discrepancies. `--eco` remains unchanged, and the deprecation of Gemini 3.1 Flash Lite remains scheduled for May 7, 2027 at the earliest.

  - **Tooling.** `build`, which builds the proven wheel before each release, joins the declared development tools tracked by Dependabot: a fresh environment was failing this check.

Re-validated on agy 1.5.9, which now launches headless sessions in Auto mode: the translation agent remains tool-free.

  - **`--use_gemini` switches to Gemini 4 Hydrogen.** Released on November 12, it is the only Gemini 4 in the free tier, and Google published a migration guide from Gemini 3.8 Flash to it. Measured on November 15 on this README in Japanese, at the same time: identical structure for both, in 72 seconds versus 108. Measured on November 15 on this README in Japanese: four out of four translations without discrepancies for each, whereas GPT-5.6 Luna, the previous eco model, lost bold formatting once out of six times. GPT-6.1 Terra is slower than GPT-5.6 Terra: 190 to 245 seconds, versus 116. OpenAI has announced no deprecation for GPT-5.6 models, which remain accepted via `--model`. `--use_codex --eco` also switches to GPT-6 Luna, measured once on subscription: this README in Japanese, without discrepancies, in 301 seconds.

  - **Reasoning effort is sent to GPT-6 and Grok models.** aipmt previously only configured reasoning for GPT-5 models: a GPT-6 or Grok via API key reasoned at its default level on every segment, including `--eco`. Measured on a twenty-line guide: Grok 4.7 consumed 695 reasoning tokens for 113 translation tokens, and Grok 4.3, the model for `--eco`, 373 for 103. GPT-6 Luna, without configuration, lost inline code or bold formatting three out of seven times; with reasoning turned off, it yielded four out of four READMEs without discrepancies. aipmt now sends reasoning turned off in `--eco`, medium effort to GPT models, and low effort to Grok models: on this README, Grok 4.7 yields the same structure at low effort, in 190 seconds, as at medium effort, in 711. A model that rejects disabled reasoning, such as Grok 4.7 or GPT-6.1 Sol, receives low effort instead of falling back to its default, most expensive setting. The xAI rejection, whose message is simply "Error code: 400", is now detected in the error body. `--reasoning_effort` still forces a specific value.

Measured on this README in Japanese: identical structure, in 190 seconds, whereas Grok 4.6 takes 372 at medium effort. `--eco` keeps Grok 4.3, the only Grok model that accepts disabled reasoning: three out of three written translations in about 3 minutes, two of which had an extra inline code; without this setting, 1.18.0 wrote one out of two, in 5 minutes, the other being rejected due to lost code.

Those no longer served by Antigravity are removed.

- **1.18.0** Gemini switches to 3.8 Flash, retries transient outages, and no longer waits indefinitely; Python 3.11 minimum and Python 3.15 tested; the new major Gemini SDK release is validated (2026-11-15):

  - **`--use_gemini` switches to Gemini 3.8 Flash.** Google deprecated Gemini 3.7 Flash on October 8: it disappeared from AI Studio, and its endpoint serves Gemini 3.8 Flash instead. aipmt's default therefore named a model that was no longer the one doing the translation. On November 14, on the free API tier, about twenty calls sent to 3.7 Flash exhausted the daily quota of 3.8 Flash. Pricing and reasoning levels remain unchanged, and `--model gemini-3.7-flash` remains accepted. Measured on November 15 on the free tier, a short file translated twice with an identical structure: 3.8 Flash rejects `minimal` reasoning, like 3.7, and accepts the next level, but took 4 then 8 minutes, when Google reported it under heavy demand; the previous evening, `--eco` responded in one second. The model for `--eco`, Gemini 3.1 Flash Lite, will be retired on May 7, 2027: its designated replacement, Gemini 3.5 Flash Lite, will be evaluated before taking its place.

  - **A transient Gemini outage no longer costs the file.** Google's SDK does not retry anything by default: at the first "503 high demand", aipmt abandoned the file, whereas the same outage has been retried on Antigravity since 1.17.1. A 503, 429, or timeout now triggers up to three retries, after 10, 20, then 40 seconds. An exhausted prepaid credit or a rejected request still fails immediately.

  - **An unresponsive Gemini call no longer hangs aipmt.** The SDK used to wait indefinitely: on November 14, a translation remained stalled for over five minutes on a silent server until it was stopped. Each attempt is now timed out after 5 minutes, then retried as a transient outage: an unresponsive server causes the segment to fail in about twenty minutes at most. For this, aipmt requires a recent enough Gemini SDK version to retry timeouts, and pip updates it as needed.

  - **Gemini retries are visible.** Google's SDK logs its retries to a logger that no one displays: a four-minute wait on a small file was indistinguishable from a freeze. Each retry is now displayed, showing the error and the wait time: on November 15, a "504 DEADLINE_EXCEEDED" after 5 minutes was successfully retried 10 seconds later. Warnings already displayed by the SDK continue to be shown.

  - **The new major release of google-genai, validated with a real-world call.** This SDK no longer provides `httpx`, which became optional, and switches to `httpx2`. aipmt imports neither, and its translation call remains unchanged. A Markdown guide translated into `--eco` by `gemini-3.1-flash-lite`, with this version, preserves a structure identical to the source. A single test was constructing an Anthropic error using `httpx`: under Python 3.11 and 3.12, nothing installed it anymore, and the suite failed on import. It now uses `httpx2`, like the Anthropic and Mistral SDKs.

  - **Python 3.11 becomes the minimum supported version.** Python 3.10 is no longer maintained since October 1, 2026, and the new major version of google-genai, released on November 6, only installs on Python 3.11+; six other packages in the pinned versions also require it. Under Python 3.10, pip now only installs 1.17.1, the last version to support it.

  - **Python 3.15 is tested.** The test suite runs on every Python version, from 3.11 to 3.15, with the package installed and its dependencies taken at their public boundaries. SonarCloud analysis, dependency checks, and PyPI publishing run under Python 3.15.

  - **Dependencies.** In the pinned versions, `anthropic`, `openai`, and `mistralai` received their minor updates, proposed by Dependabot and merged automatically. `pyasn1`, brought in by Google authentication, received fixes for four high-severity security alerts: quadratic-time decodings that malicious input could stall for an extended duration. Installing from PyPI was already pulling the patched version. `httpx` and `httpcore` are removed from pinned versions, as no package requires them anymore. The GitHub Actions for Python setup, PyPI publishing, and SonarCloud analysis were updated manually.

  - **`--use_grok_cli` translates again, using Grok 4.7.** Since version 1.0.52, the Grok CLI rejects Grok 4.6 and Grok 4.5, aipmt's quality and `--eco` models ("unknown model id"): every translation failed on the very first segment. On October 19, Grok 4.6 was still translating. Grok 4.7 becomes the default and, in the absence of a second model on the subscription, `--eco` has no effect on this path. The xAI API still serves Grok 4.6 and Grok 4.3: `--use_grok` remains unchanged. Re-run on the 1.17.0 test set, a guide and a short `--news` whose English quotation contains code: three out of three translations succeeded, matching results from other providers.

  - **Antigravity retries transient service outages.** On October 20, agy returned on the fourth segment of a CHANGELOG "API error (attempt 2): UNAVAILABLE (code 503)", after retrying once internally. Nothing indicated that the failure was retryable: aipmt abandoned the file, having consumed the quota of the first three segments, even though a manual retry succeeded. Codes 502, 503, and 504, which agy already retries on its side, now cause the segment to be retried after 30 then 60 seconds. An exhausted quota window remains an immediate failure, and when agy itself reports whether the failure is retryable, it decides.

  - **Grok CLI preflight no longer mistakes a token refresh for a logout.** Before translating, aipmt verifies the session via `grok models`, which consumes nothing. However, the call that refreshes an expired token itself returns "You are not authenticated.", while the subsequent call succeeds: aipmt used to advise logging back in via `grok login` when simply retrying was enough. It now queries again five seconds later before concluding. Observed on October 19, reproduced on the 21st.

  - **Retry notices state the reason.** Codex, Grok CLI, Antigravity, OpenCode, and Claude Code were announcing every retry as a "rate limit", including 503s: leading users to suspect quota issues. The notice now quotes the error returned by the CLI.

  - **Dependencies.** In the pinned versions, `openai` received its minor update, and `certifi` received a fix for a critical security alert, removing emSign roots whose intermediate keys were compromised. Installing from PyPI was already pulling the patched version.

- **1.17.0** Claude 5.5 via API, Codex on GPT-6.1-Sol: Sonnet 5.5, which rejected disabled thinking, becomes translatable and becomes the default model, Haiku 5.5 takes `--eco` mode; Codex switches to GPT-6.1-Sol and no longer discards a segment completed after reconnecting; Dependabot also runs over the weekend (2026-10-20):

  - **`--use_claude` translates with Claude Sonnet 5.5, Opus 5.5, and Fable 5.1.** aipmt used to disable Claude's thinking via `thinking: {"type": "disabled"}`, which these three models reject: HTTP 400 on the very first segment, verified by an actual call. aipmt now sends the lowest setting accepted by each model, determined from the capabilities published by the Anthropic API: `between_tools` for Sonnet 5.5, which disables its preliminary thinking, and no setting for Opus 5.5 and Fable 5.x, which then think at their discretion. An unrecognized model tries, on the first rejection, settings from the most economical to the most permissive, then keeps whichever succeeds for subsequent segments—the logic already used for Gemini. Any other error bubbles up as-is. All four 5.x models are added to the context window table at one million tokens.

  - **Claude Sonnet 5.5 becomes the default model for `--use_claude`.** Measured by aipmt itself on the two benchmark documents from the README — the dense monitoring article, in `--news`, and this README in its 1.14.0 revision —, fourteen languages each: 28 translations out of 28 written and without structural discrepancies, no retried segments, internal links intact, English quotes copied as-is and, in English, French translation lines removed by the model itself. Claude Sonnet 5 scored 11 and 12 out of 14 via the API on a longer revision of this README, and 13 and 13 via Claude Code. Pricing remains unchanged: $2 and $10 per million input and output tokens, amounting to $9.21 for all 28 translations.

  - **`--eco` switches to Claude Haiku 5.5, thinking included.** Measured on the same documents: with thinking disabled, Haiku 5.5 leaves entire paragraphs in French, which the untranslated excerpts guard rejects — 9 translations written out of 28. With its adaptive thinking, it writes 26, including 23 without discrepancies; two READMEs are rejected, in Arabic and Hindi, due to inline code lost on return. Claude Haiku 4.5, the previous default, measured for the first time via the API, writes all 28 but only 21 without discrepancies: on the article, it promotes a heading to level 1 across seven languages, and leaves it in French in Japanese, which no guard catches. Haiku 5.5 costs $0.57 for the 28 translations compared to $3.67, and runs twice as fast; aipmt therefore lets it think.

  - **Codex no longer drops a segment it finishes after reconnecting.** When the server responds with a 429, `codex exec` reconnects on its own and announces each retry in its JSON stream with an event of type `error`, "Reconnecting... 2/5", before resuming the turn, which can succeed. aipmt previously mistook this first `error` for a failure and abandoned the file: three of the fourteen failures during a regeneration of this repository on October 19 carried this message. Only the end of the turn is now authoritative, `turn.completed` or `turn.failed`, as designed in the Codex 0.162.1 source code. A 429 that exhausted Codex's retries, which arrives only as plain text, is also recognized: aipmt waits and then retries the segment instead of abandoning the file. An exhausted subscription quota remains an immediate failure.

  - **`--use_codex` switches to GPT-6.1-Sol.** It is the first model in the catalog that Codex serves to a ChatGPT account, and aipmt still preferred GPT-5.6-Sol over it. Measured on October 19 on this CHANGELOG in German, one translation per model, launched at the same time: GPT-6.1-Sol in 527 seconds, segments ranging from 46 to 70 seconds, identical source structure; GPT-5.6-Sol in 906 seconds, served fitfully that evening, taking up to 211 seconds for a segment, and triggering 429s as soon as four translations ran in parallel. The GPT-6 family no longer triggers the "unusual model" warning. GPT-5.6-Sol remains accessible via `--model gpt-5.6-sol`, and `--eco` retains GPT-5.6-Luna.

  - **Dependabot runs every day, weekends included.** Set to `daily`, it only ran Monday through Friday: a release exceeding its three-day grace period on a Friday after the morning run had to wait until Monday, and the daily freshness check, calibrated for one run per day, would have flagged it as overdue on Sunday. On October 18, google-genai 2.30.1 escaped this by 1 hour and 35 minutes. Dependabot now follows a daily cron, and the test aligning the check's delay with its schedule counts three days for `daily`: reverting to it without extending the delay causes it to fail.

  - **The splitting purity checker is removed**, as planned since 1.13.0. It compared the package against the snapshot taken at splitting time, and every code modification had to declare its discrepancies there. Its most useful check remains: `check-release-ready.sh` rejects any Python module not tracked by git, invisible to both hooks and CI.

  - **Two defects reported by SonarCloud are resolved**: the function that arranges code tokens within a quote (`--news`), which was too complex, has been split into two, and an exception test now calls only a single function within its block.

  - **Dependencies updated since 1.16.3.** In the pinned versions, `anthropic`, `openai`, `google-genai`, `mistralai`, and `python-dotenv` received their minor and patch updates, submitted by Dependabot and merged automatically; the publishing and SonarCloud analysis actions were bumped manually. The two questions left open by automatic merging have been settled: it operates without extra permissions, and it triggers no push workflow — the daily run of `main` acts as a substitute.

- **1.16.3** End of file: an API translation preserves its source's trailing newline, and the Mistral 3.0 SDK is validated (2026-10-04):

  - **An API translation preserves the trailing newline of its source.** API providers — OpenAI, Anthropic, Gemini, Mistral — strip surrounding whitespace from the model's response. On the final segment, this cleanup would strip the source's trailing newline: a translated file without a translator note ended without a newline, which git flags and formatters fix on every run. Spotted while validating Mistral's 3.0 SDK on a guide ending with a newline whose translation lacked one. A translator note restored it, and command-line providers — Codex, Grok CLI, Antigravity, Claude Code, OpenCode — copy the end of the segment: only an API translation without a note was affected. Translations now preserve the source's trailing newline across all providers, and add none to a source that does not have one.

  - **Mistral: 3.0 SDK validated with a real call.** `mistralai` 3.0.0 replaces the HTTP library `httpx` with `httpx2`; imports and the translation call remain unchanged. A fresh install of aipmt was already receiving it, since dependencies have no upper version cap, but no real call had tested it yet: a Markdown guide translated via the Mistral API using SDK 3.0 maintains a structure identical to the source. Pinned development versions switch to this SDK, and retry tests supply it with a `httpx2` client, the one it uses in production.

- **1.16.2** Code protection: code cited inside a blockquote (`--news`) and an indented code block inside a list no longer cause the translation to be rejected for all languages (2026-10-03):

  - **Code cited inside a blockquote no longer blocks translation.** In `--news` mode, aipmt protects inline code before setting aside English blockquotes, which are restored as-is. Code placed inside a quote was therefore set aside with it: the model never saw its token, and validation flagged it as missing across all languages. The author's daily news monitoring ground to a halt on October 2 because of this: a `/plugin enable …` command quoted in a tweet caused the probe language to be rejected twice, leaving the other thirteen never launched. Tokens carried away by a blockquote are now resolved in the set-aside text. For an English target, a blockquote's French translation line is no longer passed to the model: the prompt instructed it to remove it, and the code it contained was then counted as lost even though the model had obeyed. The published output remains unchanged. Finally, blockquote restoration no longer reinterprets backslashes: a quoted `\n` stays `\n`. Verified end-to-end on the failed article: English and German translated on the first attempt by `gpt-6.1-sol`, structure identical to the published versions.

  - **An indented code block in a list is protected.** CommonMark allows an indented block fence, which is standard when documenting a numbered list step: it belongs to the list item, so it is indented. Anchored to column 0, the protection pattern missed all of them: measured on a real-world README, 8 blocks were detected out of 14. The other six went to the model as prose and came back identical, since they were commands, and the guard rejecting unchanged translations discarded the file for all languages with a message naming the symptom, never the cause. The pattern now accepts an indented closing fence, and round-tripping between protection and restoration reproduces the document identically, indentation included.

- **1.16.1** Internal links: the anchor token leaves parentheses to the link, and a translated link is no longer silently duplicated (2026-09-27):

  - **Internal links no longer break invisibly.** To protect a link to a section, `[texte](#ancre)`, aipmt replaced its parenthesized portion, parentheses included, with a token: the model received brackets followed by a bare token, appearing as a broken link, and occasionally attempted to "repair" it. Restoration then produced `[texte]((#ancre))`, a link pointing nowhere, without any guard noticing. This defect recurred on every regeneration of this repository's translations — in Spanish, Korean, and then Italian for 1.15.0, in German for 1.16.0 — with every Gemini Flash tested, and even with Claude Sonnet and Haiku. A Markdown link token now retains its parentheses: the model receives a valid link that it has no reason to fix, and restoration also accepts a token from which the model may have removed the parentheses. HTML anchors retain a bare token. Verified end-to-end with the most fragile model, `gemini-3.8-flash-low`, on a README containing a parenthesized link: without the fix, the README was rejected, three links were duplicated, and one segment was retried; with it, three out of three links remained intact with no retries. Then tested on this README in German, Japanese, and Hindi via `gemini-3.8-flash-medium`, and in German via Claude Code with `sonnet`: structure identical to the source, all three internal links intact everywhere.

  - **Tests can no longer alter the repository that executes them.** When pushed from a linked worktree, git forwards the variable designating the repository to its hooks, and the pre-push hook runs the tests: a test's `git init`, intended for a temporary directory, reset the actual repository, which was suddenly declared worktree-less. Tests that invoke git now strip any `GIT_*` variable from their environment, and a regression test pointing this variable to a dummy repository verifies this behavior.

- **1.16.0** `--use_claude_code` provider: translate against the Claude Pro or Max subscription quota via the official `claude -p` CLI — no paid tiers, and every call proves it (2026-09-26):

  - **Eleventh path: Claude subscription, without pay-per-use billing.** `--use_claude_code` controls the official Claude Code binary in `-p` mode, built on the same foundation as Codex, Grok CLI, and Antigravity — dedicated process group, clean shutdown on timeout, Ctrl-C, or terminal termination. Not to be confused with `--use_claude`, Anthropic's pay-per-use API. aipmt never reads the token.

  - **No paid paths, verified across three tiers.** The subprocess environment uses an allowlist: no API key, token, cloud provider, or marker from the Claude Code session from which aipmt might be launched — the session that authored this provider exported fifteen of them. The preflight check, without any model turn, requires `claude auth status` subscription login without a Console key, and `/usage` the phrase confirming subscription status. Every call must then confirm it within its initialization event — `apiKeySource: none`, forced environment properly received, model from the requested family, no fast mode — and within its quota report: paid "extra usage" aborts translation immediately.

  - **The quota is shared with Claude Code sessions, and the provider conserves it.** Each call reports usage for both the 5-hour and weekly windows; beyond 80% (`AIPMT_CLAUDE_MAX_UTILIZATION`), no further segments are started. On the day of writing, the author's account was already at 98% for the week.

  - **Nothing from user configuration enters, nothing from the session remains.** No tools, no personalization mode, user settings ignored, MCP servers and hooks disabled, private and disposable working directory, no transcripts saved. Measured with canaries: without `CLAUDE_CODE_DISABLE_ATTACHMENTS`, a `@chemin` in the document became an attachment, and the model returned a read call instead of the translation; with it, nothing leaks through. The `AGENTS.md`, `CLAUDE.md`, and hook canaries loaded and executed nothing.

  - **A segment starting with `/` failed to reach the model.** Under `--disable-slash-commands`, Claude Code directly responds with "... isn't available in this environment.", returning a `success` status without any turns. Prepending a newline to the segment sends it to the model; the output contract rejects in any case a response returned without a turn, across multiple turns, truncated, or by a model other than the one announced.

  - **Models: aliases, never a pinned identifier.** `sonnet` by default, with `low` effort, and in `--eco` as well — `--eco` therefore changes nothing on this path —, `opus` and `haiku` via `--model`; `fable` and the `[1m]` variants are rejected, because under `-p` they consume paid credits without prompting.

  - **Defaults are measured, not assumed.** Measured by `aipmt --use_claude_code` itself, four parallel translations, across both README benchmark documents — the dense monitoring article in `--news`, and this README at revision 1.14.0 —, fourteen languages each. `sonnet`, at `low` effort, writes all 28 translations, 26 without discrepancies: one extra bold word in Chinese on the article, and in Arabic on the README, a table row merged into the previous one, masked by rendering. Internal links intact, English quotes copied as-is, and in English, French translation lines removed by the model without invented flags; 6 min 49 s median per language on the article, 2 min 20 s on the README. `opus` translates the README without any discrepancies, 25% faster, but fails on the article: its guardrails stopped the response across all fourteen languages on a biology news brief. `haiku` writes everything — three section headings promoted to level 1 on the article, duplicate internal links in the English README —, but twice as slowly as `sonnet`: it reasons without any way to disable it (accounting for 61% of its output tokens), and its lower price yields only a 14% saving in API equivalence. Hence `sonnet` in `--eco` as well. Effort provides no benefit for translation: on an initial batch — this README and this CHANGELOG in English, Japanese, and Hindi —, `sonnet` generated zero reasoning tokens, `opus` at medium effort used 1% of its output tokens, and medium effort offered no improvement over low, either in structure or runtime. The `sonnet` run, totaling 1.8 million source characters, consumed 9 percentage points of the 5-hour window and 2 of the weekly quota on the author's Max subscription — an upper bound, as the session running the benchmarks drew from the same quota.

  - **Anthropic guardrail refusals are explicitly named as such.** Opus 5.5 features stricter guardrails than Sonnet, and Anthropic's error message warns that they sometimes flag work closely related to biological research. A tech watch brief covering 279 generated molecules for a binding site was enough to trigger it. Claude Code then cuts off the response, retries once, and the model returns "I can't continue this translation" with a success status, without any error, over two turns: only the single-turn contract stopped this response, with a silent message regarding its cause. When sent alone, the brief is rejected by the API under the "bio" category. aipmt now recognizes these signs, names the guardrails and their category, does not retry—the same text would trigger the same refusal—and suggests `--model sonnet`, which translated the brief into all fourteen languages.

  - **Translations in this repository can use this route, without an exemption.** `REGEN_PROVIDER=claude_code` is a subscription, not a billed API: it runs without `REGEN_ALLOW_PAID_API`, with four jobs—the runs operated at four concurrent translations without a single rate limit or connection lock error—after a single preflight check that consumes zero quota. The default remains Codex with `gpt-5.6-sol`.

  - **Security: a project `.env` can no longer redirect API calls.** `find_dotenv(usecwd=True)` searches for the file starting from the current directory and its parents: an untrusted tree — a freshly cloned repository — could place `OPENROUTER_BASE_URL`, `XAI_BASE_URL`, or `OPENAI_BASE_URL` (the latter read by the SDK itself) without knowing any key, and the real key, originating from the environment or user configuration, would then be sent in the authorization header to a third-party server. The filter operates on a PATTERN rather than a list: audited across installed SDKs, twelve routing variables are read, six of which by the Anthropic client alone — a hand-written enumeration would have missed half of them. Therefore, any variable matching `_BASE_URL`, `_API_BASE`, or `_ENDPOINT`, proxies, and certificate stores (pointing to a controlled CA makes an interceptor indistinguishable from a genuine server) are rejected from the project layer, as well as `XDG_CONFIG_HOME` and `APPDATA`: setting them amounted to deciding which file constitutes the user layer, thereby bypassing the filter through the back door. These variables are only accepted from the two layers controlled by the user: the exported environment and `~/.config/aipmt/.env`. Furthermore, the project layer is read WITHOUT interpolation: `load_dotenv` expands `${VAR}` by default, and an untrusted `.env` containing `NOM_ANODIN=${OPENAI_API_KEY}` copied the real key under a name that subprocess pattern filtering does not recognize — it would then enter the environment of `codex exec`, violating the stated invariant. The budget therefore comes from the text actually sent, bounded above by its UTF-8 byte count: across all byte-merging tokenizers — byte-level BPE, SentencePiece with byte fallback, the families used across the catalog — a token is worth at least one byte, and this is the only upper bound available here, where OpenRouter routes to dozens of tokenizers that are unknown in advance. No average ratio was adequate — an ideogram in the supplementary plane drops to 1.33 bytes per token, a combining character to 1.00 —, and input plus output now fit within the window by construction. A segment too dense for the selected model is rejected before the call rather than after billing.

- **1.9.2** Fix news attribution URL extraction with nested parentheses or FR prefix (2026-05-11):

  - **Bug fixed**: attribution URL extraction in `_protect_news_quotes` used the regex `re.search(r"\((.+?)\)", attribution)` (lazy capture between parentheses). On an attribution of the type `(relayé par [@user sur X](https://x.com/.../123))` (nested parentheses: outer `(` + markdown link's `]()`), the capture stopped at the first `)` encountered → truncated string + including the FR prefix: `relayé par [@user sur X](https://x.com/.../123` (without trailing `)`). Consequence: `_validate_news_post` searched for this string in the translated output and systematically failed (two reasons: truncated `)` + "relayé par" translated into `relayed by`/`weitergeleitet von`/...). Full cascade low → medium → high → gpt-5.5 could not succeed.
  - **Fix**: the regex becomes `re.search(r"\]\(([^)]+)\)", attribution)` — specifically targets the `](url)` of the markdown link, captures **only the pure URL** (without FR prefix or truncation), an invariant preserved by the `#URL{N}#` placeholders during translation. Robust against both problematic patterns:
